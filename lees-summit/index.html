How Lee's Summit Homeowners Can Extend Water Heater Life

Many premature tank failures trace back to maintenance that never happened, and the habits that prevent them are simple. Drain and flush the tank once a year to clear sediment, have the anode rod inspected every few years, and test the relief valve annually so it doesn't seize. Keep the temperature near 120 degrees for safer water and less scale. If you have a tankless unit, descale it yearly where the water is hard so the heat exchanger stays clean.

Take a look at the age of your water heater. The serial number on the label usually includes the manufacture date, and that date tells you a lot. Most tank models reach 8 to 12 years, heat pump units 10 to 15, and well-maintained tankless units about 20. If your heater is past that point and also shows rusty water, popping sounds, or dampness under the tank, it's usually due for a planned replacement, and that's almost always cheaper and less stressful than an emergency replacement after a leak.

If warning signs do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A dripping relief valve might point to excess pressure or thermal expansion with nowhere to go, both modest fixes. The same drip ignored for months can wear out the valve and stress the tank until the tank itself fails, and a small repair becomes a full replacement plus water damage. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and cold showers.

Common Water Heater Problems in Lee's Summit

Every type of water heater has its own weak points, and we work on all of them around Lee's Summit. Gas tanks usually give trouble through thermocouples and flame sensors, dirty burners, and venting that doesn't draft. Electric tanks burn out elements and thermostats, particularly when sediment buries the lower element. Tankless units throw error codes for scale in the heat exchanger, ignition faults, and flow sensor trouble, most of which trace back to skipped descaling. Heat pump water heaters need clean air filters and a clear condensate drain, and they don't do well in small, closed-in spaces. We service all four types regularly and stock the common parts to finish most repairs in one trip.
